Requirements

Section 50199.15 (a) of the California Health and Safety Code requires the California Tax
Credit Allocation Committee (CTCAC) to submit an annual report of the prior year's activities
to the California State Legislature, reporting on the following information:

    · The total amount of lower-income housing credits allocated by CTCAC;
    · The total number of lower-income units that are, or will be, assisted by the credits

         occupied by households whose income is 60 percent or less of area median gross
         income;
    · The amount of credits allocated to each project, other financing available to the
         project, and the number of units that are, or will be, occupied by households whose
         income is 60 percent or less of area median gross income; and,
    · Sufficient information to identify the project.

The annual report must include an aggregation of information submitted annually by housing
sponsors for all projects that received an allocation in previous years, specifying the following:

    · Information sufficient to identify the project;
    · The total number of units in the project; and,
    · The total number of units assisted by the credit that are required to be occupied by

         households whose income is 60 percent or less of the area median gross income as a
         condition of receiving the credit.

Finally, the annual report to the Legislature must include any recommendations for
improvement in the low-income housing tax credit and, beginning in 2025, an aggregation
of the information submitted to CTCAC pursuant to AB 2873 (Jones-Sawyer, 2022).

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Since the program's inception in 1987, CTCAC has assisted more than 550,000 affordable
units with tax credit awards, serving over 1 million lower-income households, supporting
nearly 300,000 jobs per year, and generating over $12 billion in tax revenue and $3.5 billion in
wages and business income per year.

Despite ongoing challenges facing the housing industry and the state ­ inflation, high interest
rates, natural disasters ­ CTCAC continues to receive a large volume of shovel-ready project
applications and had to turn away more than half due to limited federal 9% Low Income
Housing Tax Credits (LIHTCs) and state Low-Income Housing Tax Credits (state LIHTCs).

LIHTCs are available for new construction projects or existing properties undergoing
rehabilitation. Two types of federal tax credits are available and are generally referred to as
nine percent (9%) and four percent (4%) LIHTCs. Each number refers to the approximate
percentage that is multiplied against a project's requested "qualified basis" to determine the
maximum amount of annual federal credits CTCAC may award the project. While low-
income housing tax credits are referred to in annual terms, each federal LIHTC award earns
investors 10 years of annual federal tax credits.

Set-Asides

Consistent with federal and state law, CTCAC sets aside ten percent (10%) of the available
9% tax credits for non-profit entities. Within the non-profit set-aside, CTCAC regulations
prioritize projects that designate at least 50% of the lower-income units for homeless
households. State law also provides that 20% of federal credits be set aside for allocation to
rural projects. CTCAC regulations provide for a 4% set-aside for special needs developments,
a 5% set-aside for affordable housing at risk of losing affordability, and that 10% of the rural
set aside be available for projects on land owned by a federally-recognized Indian tribe.

4% LOW-INCOME HOUSING TAX CREDITS

The federal 4% LIHTCs derive from a project's use of tax-exempt private activity bonds (PABs)
allocated by the California Debt Limit Allocation Committee (CDLAC) and are limited only
by the amount of bonds annually available in California. CTCAC and CDLAC analysts work
collaboratively to review the bond and tax credit applications to ensure all federal and state
requirements are met and that each project will contribute to the housing goals set by the
State of California and the Treasurer's Office. The 4% applications at CTCAC are reviewed
and scored competitively through the CDLAC scoring system. Only projects successful in the
CDLAC competition will receive federal 4% LIHTCs.
In July 2025, H.R. 1 was signed into law, which, among other things, permanently reduced the
bond financing threshold from 50% to 25%. Practically speaking, this change will allow
California to finance nearly double the amount of affordable housing using PABs and federal
4% LIHTCs. California was the first state to begin implementing this change, with CDLAC and
CTCAC adopting emergency regulations on August 5, just one month after the federal
legislation was enacted.
As a result of these swift changes, in 2025, CTCAC allocated $639,918,711 in federal 4% LIHTCs
to 195 projects, totaling 25,476 lower-income units, over 10,000 more units than in 2024.

Berryessa TOD, which received CDLAC bonds and federal 4% LIHTCs in 2025, is the first phase of the Berryessa BART Urban Village Plan ­ a
comprehensive, multi-phase redevelopment initiative designed to create a vibrant, transit-rich, and pedestrian-friendly neighborhood centered
around the Berryessa BART Station. Berryessa TOD will include 193 affordable units, 49 of which will be permanent supportive housing units for
previously unhoused individuals or families with special needs. The site is one of several sites owned by Valley Transportation Authority (VTA) that
are actively being converted from park-and-ride lots into affordable housing. (Photo credit: Affirmed Housing)

STATE LOW-INCOME HOUSING TAX CREDITS

Recognizing the high cost of developing housing in California, the State Legislature created
the state low-income housing tax credit (state LIHTC) program in 1987 to augment the
federal LIHTC program. State LIHTCs are only available to projects that previously received, or
are concurrently receiving, an allocation of federal LIHTCs. The 2025 state credit ceiling of
$126,125,128 less $53,468,706 2024 in advance allocations in prior years resulted in an
available amount of $72,656,422 for 2025. Of the annual statutory state credit ceiling, 15% is
available to be combined with tax-exempt bond financed acquisition/rehabilitation projects
applying for federal 4% LIHTCs and the remaining balance is allocated through the federal
9% LIHTC geographic apportionments.
The state also provides $500,000 in state farmworker tax credits annually, which was not
allocated in 2025.
In 2025, CTCAC allocated $124,722,178 in statutory state credits to 17 federal 9% LIHTC
projects and 1 federal 4% acquisition/rehab LIHTC project, totaling 976 lower-income units.

MOSA, a 387 unit affordable housing development in Elk Grove held its grand opening in 2025. Eight three-story and six four-story buildings make
up the community along with learning centers, a fitness studio, courtyards and community lounges, recreation and play areas and surface
parking. MOSA was one of the first projects financed from CDLAC's BIPOC pool, and received over $70 million in enhanced state tax credits.

Since 2020, the state budget has provided an additional $500 million in state low-income
housing tax credits (enhanced state LIHTCs) to be combined with federal 4% LIHTCs for new
construction multifamily housing projects. The additional $500,000,000, plus a carry forward of
$4,166,150 from 2024, totaled $504,166,150 in available enhanced state LIHTCs. In 2025, $100

STATE HISTORIC REHABILITATION TAX
CREDIT

Senate Bill 451 (Chapter 703, Statutes of 2019) established the State Historic Rehabilitation Tax
Credit (SHRTC) program to allow a taxpayer that receives a tax credit allocation, subject to
appropriation, to claim a credit against those taxes for each taxable year beginning on or
after January 1, 2021, and before January 1, 2027, for the rehabilitation of certified historic
structures. The state appropriated $50 million for the program in 2022, with $8 million set aside
for taxpayers with qualified rehabilitation expenditures of less than $1 million for any other
certified historic building that is not a qualified residence and $2 million for taxpayers with
qualified rehabilitation expenditures for a certified historic structure that is a qualified
residence. The remainder was made available on July 1, 2025, for taxpayers with qualified
rehabilitation expenditures of more than $1 million for affordable housing projects that had
previously applied and were next in line for funding.

CTCAC administers the SHRTC in coordination with the Office of Historic Preservation (OHP)
Division of the California State Parks. The Office of Administrative Law approved CTCAC's
Regulations in 2024 and in January 2025, OHP opened applications for the credits. CTCAC
allocated the credits on a first-come, first-served basis upon OHP's approval of a project's
application.

CTCAC awarded a total of 12 SHRTC projects in 2025, exhausting the $50 million available.

CTCAC COMPLIANCE

As required by federal law, CTCAC monitors a tax credit project for progress in meeting
milestones and reservation requirements up until it is completed and "placed in service."
Additionally, Internal Revenue Code Section 42 and state statutes require CTCAC to monitor
compliance throughout the entire term of the project's regulatory period. CTCAC is required
to monitor projects after they have been "placed-in-service" and every three years
thereafter during the year federal credit compliance period. For the remaining term of the
regulatory agreement, ranging from 30 years for older projects to 55 years for current
projects, CTCAC is solely responsible for enforcement and monitoring projects on a five-year
schedule. When CTCAC monitors a project, it inspects and reviews at least 20% of the files
and residential units.

CTCAC's compliance monitoring program must determine, among other requirements,
whether the income of families residing in lower-income units and the rents they are charged
are within agreed upon limits stated in the regulatory agreement. CTCAC staff must conduct
physical inspections of units and buildings in each development to ensure they are in safe,
sanitary, and habitable condition. Project owners are also required to report complete and
accurate project information to CTCAC annually. All non-compliance with federal
requirements or reporting failures during the federal credit compliance period, whether
corrected or not, is required to be reported to the Internal Revenue Service (IRS). CTCAC
Compliance completed 1,231 on-site audits in 2025, totaling over 16,081 units. During the 15-
year federal compliance period, the IRS may recapture federal tax credits from owners for
findings of non-compliance. Thereafter, and for violations of state requirements that exceed
federal standards, CTCAC may issue negative points to owners, levy fines, or pursue legal
action.

In addition to performing compliance monitoring, CTCAC staff conducts annual basic and
advanced compliance workshops for owners, developers, investors, management company
agents, and asset managers. In 2025, the CTCAC staff held a total of 5 compliance
workshops virtually to stakeholders, including owners, tax credit investors and property
managers, to provide updates on CTCAC and the program generally. Over 30 hours of
training was provided by CTCAC staff to the 979 participants who attended these workshops.

As required by AB 2006 (Berman, 2022), CTCAC, the Department of Housing and Community
Development (HCD), and the California Housing Finance Agency (CalHFA) entered into a
Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) in July 2024 between the three agencies to

LOOKING FORWARD

Federal Changes
H.R. 1 was signed into law in July 2025, increasing the federal 9% tax credit authority by 12%
annually and reducing the bond funding threshold for projects to qualify for 4% LIHTCs from
50% to 25%. These two changes will increase the number of projects awarded by (1)
increasing available federal 9% LIHTCs and (2) more efficiently using tax-exempt bond
authority, potentially doubling the number of projects awarded federal 4% LIHTCs.
Novogradac, a national affordable housing and tax credit consulting firm, estimates these
two changes would increase the amount of LIHTC units developed in California by over
20,000 per year. Following quick implementation of reduced 25% standard, CTCAC awarded
more than double the anticipated amount of federal 4% LIHTCs than expected in the third
round of 2025, resulting in 10,000 more LIHTC units for 2025 compared to 2024, on pace to
exceed the estimated 20,000 annual increase.

Demand

Demand for tax-exempt bond financing and, by extension, federal 4% LIHTCs for new
construction projects is highly dependent on the availability of enhanced state LIHTCs and
other state and local "soft" funding sources. The drop in new construction in 2023 was largely
due to projects' reliance on enhanced state credits. While demand for the enhanced state
credits is still high - $1.3 billion in requests for $500 million available ­ many projects were
restructured to fill their financing gaps with other available "soft" funding sources in 2024 and
2025, increasing the new construction numbers close to the same levels as 2021 and 2022.

CDLAC's swift implementation of the 25% standard meant CDLAC and CTCAC were able to

Annual Rent Cap Policy

Pursuant to AB 846 (Bonta, 2024) and effective January 1, 2025, CTCAC implemented a rent
increase limit on all low-income housing tax credit projects with a CTCAC regulatory
agreement. Under the rent cap, owners may increase gross rent for a lower-income
household provided that in any 12-month period the increase does not exceed the lesser of
5% plus the percentage increase in the cost of living or 10% of the lowest rental rate charged
for that household at any time during the 12 months prior to the effective date of the
increase.

Upon request, the Executive Director may waive the rent cap limit if the project owner shows
that the proposed increase is necessary to ensure the financial stability or fiscal integrity of
the project and does not unreasonably impact existing residents.

The annual rent cap is in addition to the general program maximum rents for that are
published by the Department of Housing and Urban Development each year.

Affordable Housing Preservation

To help to preserve the affordability of rent restricted multifamily rental housing and provide
orderly, early warnings and purchase opportunities to keep units affordable when restrictions
or subsidies expire, CTCAC developed and is implementing new processes to better track
data and share information with the California Housing and Community Development's
Housing Preservation Unit.
